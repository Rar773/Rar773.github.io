Bonds Rout Deepens as S&P Posts Worst Day Since March, Oil Surges Above $109
🕐 22:11 · May 15, 2026
$SPY
$QQQ
$OIL
A widespread selloff in bond markets dragged equities lower, abruptly interrupting the AI-fueled rally. The S&P 500 fell 1.22%, marking the largest single-day decline since March. Nasdaq dropped 1.51% as chipmakers — which had led the recent rally — gave back gains.

US 10-year Treasury yields surpassed 4.5% while Japan's 30-year debt touched 4% for the first time since 1999. In the United Kingdom, a political crisis pushed long-bond rates to a 28-year high, with the 10-year gilt yield jumping 15 basis points to 5.14%.

"The dollar extended its weekly gain. US crude settled above $105. With no end in sight to the Iran dispute, suspicion has grown that an effective closing of the Strait of Hormuz will exacerbate energy difficulties."

Brent crude rose to $109.26 per barrel, up $3.54 or 3.35%, as Iran's Foreign Minister warned that all vessels except those "at war with us" could pass through the Strait of Hormuz — a waterway carrying 20% of global oil supply.

Technology shares led declines across Europe and Asia. South Korea's Kospi tumbled 6.1% as investors took profits in Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix. Nvidia fell 2.3% in premarket after a seven-session winning streak.
